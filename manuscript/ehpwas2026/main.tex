\begin{abstract}
Medical federated learning (FL) must jointly address gradient inversion, Byzantine poisoning, and Harvest-Now-Decrypt-Later (HNDL) risk under long clinical retention. We present \textbf{ZKFL-PQ}, a three-layer architecture that combines NIST ML-KEM-768 transport, a lattice-based zero-knowledge proof (ZKP) of $\ell_2$-norm boundedness bound to the BFV ciphertext (with an Enc-consistency gadget for encryption coins), and BFV homomorphic aggregation opened by $(t,n)$-threshold \emph{partial} decryption without reconstructing the secret key. An Unruh-style NIZK (default $r{=}128$) targets QROM-oriented soundness; a public artifact realizes full-vector packing, Classic-128 HE presets with a fused SEAL+threshold path, default post-ZKP median aggregation, and Lean-/EasyCrypt-backed combinatorial Unruh lemmas. Empirically we report multi-seed synthetic baselines (including sign-flip), a scale study with $N{=}20$ and $T{=}30$, UCI Breast Cancer and full-resolution PneumoniaMNIST runs, communication payloads, and a backdoor evaluation with a complementary median aggregator. We position ZKFL-PQ against Beskar-class secure aggregation without claiming replacement.
\end{abstract}

Post-quantum cryptography, federated learning, zero-knowledge proofs, homomorphic encryption, medical AI, Byzantine robustness

\section{Introduction}
Federated learning (FL)~\cite{mcmahan2017fedavg} keeps raw records at the edge, yet model updates remain vulnerable to gradient inversion~\cite{zhu2019deep}, Byzantine poisoning~\cite{blanchard2017machine}, and HNDL attacks against classical TLS when medical archives outlive RSA/ECC~\cite{mosca2018cybersecurity}. Hospital consortia therefore need (i)~post-quantum (PQ) session confidentiality, (ii)~verifiable rejection of oversized updates, and (iii)~aggregation that does not place a single curious server in sole possession of plaintext gradients.

Clinical deployments retain data for years to decades, operate under multi-institutional governance (no single root of trust), and face regulators that ask how updates are authenticated and who can decrypt them. Wrapping FedAvg in classical TLS is insufficient against adversaries that store ciphertexts today and open them later~\cite{mosca2018cybersecurity}.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item A three-layer \textbf{ZKFL-PQ} protocol: ML-KEM-768, ciphertext-bound norm ZKP with language $\mathcal{L}_{\tau}^{\mathrm{bind}}$ and Enc-consistency for BFV coins $\rho$, full-vector BFV, and $(t,n)$ partial-threshold opening.
  \item Concrete parameters, quantization, noise-budget reasoning, Unruh-style NIZK ($r{=}128$ default), and per-round communication accounting suited to WiMob/eHPWAS.
  \item A public artifact~\cite{zkflcode} that encrypts all coordinates, accepts updates by cryptographic verification only, and never assembles a monolithic server $sk$ at decrypt time.
  \item An empirical study: multi-seed synthetic baselines under large-norm and sign-flip; scale $N{=}20$, $T{=}30$; UCI Breast Cancer and full-resolution MedMNIST; backdoor ASR with a complementary median defense.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Threat Model and Claims}
\textbf{Adversary:} (i)~network eavesdropper with future quantum computers against factoring/DLP; (ii)~up to $f{<}N/2$ Byzantine clients; (iii)~curious aggregator that sees protocol messages but does not hold $sk$ alone; (iv)~up to $t{-}1$ colluding threshold decryptors.

\textbf{Claims:} ML-KEM-768 transport (FIPS~203 Level~3)~\cite{nist_fips203_2024}; norm soundness for oversized witnesses under SIS + (Unruh/)RO assumptions; ciphertext binding so a proof cannot be replayed on a different $\mathbf{ct}$; aggregator privacy via Shamir-shared $sk$ and partial decryption $\mu_i=c_1\star(\lambda_i\cdot s_i)$.

\textbf{Scope.} We do not claim differential privacy or a drop-in replacement for Beskar-class full-stack secure aggregation~\cite{zhang2025beskar}. Pure $\ell_2$ gates do not stop sign-flip or in-bound backdoors; we measure those regimes and compose with robust aggregation when needed.

\section{Protocol Design}

\subsection{Round Structure}
Each round $t$: the server broadcasts $w^{(t)}$. Client $i$ runs local SGD on $D_i$, quantizes to $\tilde{w}_i$, encrypts under BFV public key $pk$, proves $\norm{\tilde{w}_i}_2\le\tau$ bound to $\mathbf{ct}_i$ (and Enc-consistency of coins), and sends an ML-KEM-wrapped payload. The server verifies proofs, sums accepted ciphertexts, and threshold-opens the aggregate (Algorithm~\ref{alg:target}).

\subsection{Quantization}
$\mathrm{Quantize}$ clips coordinates to $[-c,c]$, scales by $s$, and rounds into $\mathbb{Z}_t$ (artifact defaults $c{=}1$, $s{=}10^{3}$). The public threshold $\tau$ applies after quantization; adaptive rules $\tau^{(t)}=\hat\mu{+}k\hat\sigma$ are compatible. Reported runs use fixed $\tau$ (synthetic $5$; medical $8$--$12$).

\subsection{Bound Norm Language}
\begin{definition}[Bound language]
Let $C=\Commit(\tilde{w};\mathbf{r})=\mathbf{A}(\tilde{w}\|\mathbf{r})\bmod q'$
and $\mathbf{ct}=\Enc_{pk}(\tilde{w};\rho)$. Then
\begin{multline}
\mathcal{L}_{\tau}^{\mathrm{bind}}=
\bigl\{(C,\mathbf{ct},\tau):\exists(\tilde{w},\mathbf{r},\rho)\\
\quad C=\Commit(\tilde{w};\mathbf{r}),\
\mathbf{ct}=\Enc(\tilde{w};\rho),\
\norm{\tilde{w}}_2\le\tau\bigr\}.
\end{multline}
\end{definition}

\paragraph{$\Sigma$-protocol~\cite{lyubashevsky2012lattice}.}
Commit masks $\mathbf{y}$ and message $\tilde{w}$; the Fiat--Shamir challenge is $e=H(C\|\mathbf{ct}\|T\|\tau)$; responses $(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{r}_z)$ satisfy $T+eC\equiv\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{z}\|\mathbf{r}_z)$ and $\norm{\mathbf{z}}_2\le B$. Rejection sampling yields HVZK in the classical ROM.

\paragraph{Unruh-oriented NIZK.}
Classical FS is not tightly QROM-secure~\cite{kiltz2018qrom}. We use parallel binary Unruh sessions (library default $r{=}128$; latency-sensitive demos often $32$--$64$) with invertible-RO records. Combinatorial uniqueness / $2^{-r}$ lemmas are machine-checked in Lean~4; executable game hops and an in-repository EasyCrypt QROM interface (\texttt{formal/easycrypt/QROM.ec}) accompany the artifact.

\paragraph{Enc-consistency.}
Beyond hashing $\mathbf{ct}$ into the challenge, a dedicated Σ-gadget proves knowledge of coins $\rho=(u,e_0,e_1)$ such that the BFV encryption equations hold for the same plaintext chunk, closing split attacks that pair a benign proof with a mismatched ciphertext.

\bottomrule

Additive BFV yields $\Dec(\sum_i\Enc(m_i))=\sum_i m_i$ under a noise budget growing roughly as $N\cdot O(\sigma\sqrt{n})$. After key generation, secret coefficients are Shamir-shared and the monolithic $sk$ is erased. Opening uses Lagrange-weighted partial decryptions with smudging: $s$ is never reconstructed for production decrypt. An optional TenSEAL/SEAL sidecar runs in the same process as threshold BFV (\texttt{FusedSealThresholdHE}): primary open remains partial-decrypt, while SEAL provides a certified encoder path and consistency check.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{ZKFL-PQ round}
\label{alg:target}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $w^{(t)}$, $\tau$, KEM $(ek_S,dk_S)$, HE $pk$, decryptor shares
\State Broadcast $w^{(t)}$
\For{each client $i$}
  \State $\tilde{w}\gets\mathrm{Quantize}(\mathrm{LocalSGD}(w^{(t)},D_i))$
  \State $\mathbf{ct}_i\gets\Enc_{pk}(\tilde{w};\rho_i)$; prove Enc-consistency of $\rho_i$
  \State $\pi_i\gets\Prove_{\mathrm{Unruh}}((\mathbf{ct}_i,\tau),\tilde{w})$
  \State send ML-KEM-wrapped $(\mathbf{ct}_i,\pi_i)$
\EndFor
\State Keep $i$ iff cryptographic verify returns $1$
\State $\mathbf{ct}^{\star}\gets\sum_{i\in S}\mathbf{ct}_i$; threshold-open; $w^{(t+1)}\gets w^{(t)}+\mathrm{mean}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{theorem}[Sketch]
Under SIS binding of $\Commit$, RLWE hardness of BFV, and RO modeling of $H$, the NIZK is complete for honest $\norm{\tilde{w}}_2\le\tau$ and sound against oversized witnesses except with $\approx 2^{-r}$ (binary Unruh) plus SIS advantage. Threshold privacy holds against the aggregator and any $t{-}1$ decryptors.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Implementation.}
The public repository~\cite{zkflcode} realizes Algorithm~\ref{alg:target} end-to-end: shared dimension for prove and encrypt, ciphertext-bound challenges, no client-supplied acceptance Booleans, full-vector packing, Enc-consistency, and threshold partial decrypt. Scripts pin seeds and emit JSON for reproduction.

\subsection{Scale, Medical, and Backdoor Studies}
A compact-model scale study with $N{=}20$ clients and $T{=}30$ rounds reproduces the same qualitative pattern under both attacks: ZKP and hybrid reject oversized updates with high probability, while sign-flip still requires a robust aggregator. Wall-clock remains interactive on CPU for the compact MLP used in that study, which is the intended operating point for validating the crypto pipeline before scaling encoders.

On UCI Breast Cancer Wisconsin (569 samples, 30 clinical features; compact MLP with $d{=}1554$), the target stack---Unruh with $r{=}64$ (library default $128$), full-vector HE over four degree-$512$ chunks, and $(2,3)$ partial threshold decrypt---yields accuracies $88.6\%\!\rightarrow\!93.0\%\!\rightarrow\!\mathbf{93.9\%}$ across three rounds while rejecting a scripted oversized client (Fig.~\ref{fig:med}). Mean round time is $\approx 4$--$5$\,s and per-round payload $\approx 1.4$--$1.7$\,MB on a laptop CPU. Full-resolution PneumoniaMNIST uses native $28\times 28$ images ($784$ pixels, no learned or random projection), a compact head, and $25$ HE chunks; accuracy reaches about $67.5\%$ by round three under the same cryptographic stack, with oversized updates rejected.

Trigger backdoors whose poisoned updates remain inside $\tau$ illustrate why the default artifact composition pairs cryptographic accept with coordinate-wise median aggregation (Multi-Krum selectable). Pure $\ell_2$ ZKPs accept such updates by design; the post-ZKP robust step reduces attack success rate relative to FedAvg while preserving clean accuracy.

\section{Security Discussion}
Honest clients with $\norm{\tilde{w}}_2\le\tau$ produce Unruh sessions that pass algebraic and norm checks except for rejection-sampling aborts (bounded retries in the artifact). If $\norm{\tilde{w}}_2\gg\tau$, sessions with challenge bit $1$ fail with overwhelming probability under the Gaussian mask design; soundness therefore depends on the fraction of $1$-challenges across $r$ repetitions. The library default $r{=}128$ targets a $\approx 128$-bit combinatorial Unruh class; demos may reduce $r$ for CPU latency at linear proof-size cost. Binding $H$ to ciphertext bytes, together with the Enc-consistency gadget for $\rho$, prevents a benign proof from being attached to a different ciphertext. BFV semantic security (RLWE) hides individual updates from parties lacking $sk$; with Shamir $(t,n)$, the aggregator and any $t{-}1$ decryptors cannot open. We do not claim differential privacy; sites that need DP can compose mechanisms as in Beskar~\cite{zhang2025beskar}.

\paragraph{Layering.}
ML-KEM protects payloads in transit. The ZKP protects aggregation logic against oversized Byzantine updates. BFV with threshold opening protects gradient privacy against a curious server and against $t{-}1$ share holders. None of the three layers alone meets the joint medical threat model; the contribution is their composition with explicit binding.

\paragraph{Deployment.}
In a hospital consortium, each site runs a client; a neutral coordinator verifies proofs and sums ciphertexts; $t$-of-$n$ share holders (for example a DPO and two hospital CISOs) jointly open aggregates on a schedule. Daily or weekly training absorbs the observed $\sim$20$\times$ CPU overhead of the hybrid stack relative to plaintext FedAvg on the synthetic workload (Fig.~\ref{fig:break}). For imaging-scale $d$, HE chunk counts and Unruh proof sizes grow with $d$; starting with tabular or compact models (UCI demo) remains a practical way to validate the crypto pipeline before scaling encoders.

\section{Limitations}
The default artifact path already composes Unruh/ZKP accept with median aggregation, runs fused SEAL+threshold HE in one process when TenSEAL is installed, and ships a stdlib-free EasyCrypt QROM interface for Unruh theories. Remaining research-grade work includes a full machine-checked proof of SHA3 instantiations inside a production EasyCrypt QROM library, and larger imaging CNNs without compact heads. These do not remove the concrete guarantees of binding, full-vector HE, threshold privacy, and robust post-filters realized by default.

\section{Conclusion}
ZKFL-PQ composes ML-KEM-768, ciphertext-bound Unruh norm proofs with Enc-consistency, and threshold BFV partial decryption for quantum-resilient medical FL. Across synthetic, scale, tabular, and imaging experiments---and with explicit honesty on sign-flip and backdoors---we show a budgetable, reproducible path to verifiable PQ aggregation for WiMob/eHPWAS settings.
