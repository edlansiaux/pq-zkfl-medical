\begin{abstract}
Medical federated learning (FL) must jointly address gradient inversion, Byzantine poisoning, and Harvest-Now-Decrypt-Later (HNDL) risk under long clinical retention. We present \textbf{ZKFL-PQ}, a three-layer architecture combining NIST ML-KEM-768 transport, a lattice-based zero-knowledge proof (ZKP) of $\ell_2$-norm boundedness bound to the BFV ciphertext, and BFV homomorphic aggregation with $(t,n)$-threshold decryption that opens via Lagrange-weighted partial decryptions (no reconstructed $sk$). Relative to the submission draft, the camera-ready artifact and manuscript close the issues raised by eHPWAS reviewers: ciphertext-bound Fiat--Shamir (no trusted client Booleans), full-vector HE packing, threshold BFV without a monolithic server $sk$, an Unruh-style QROM-oriented NIZK (default $r{=}128$), Classic-128 HE presets with an optional lattice-estimator reporter, multi-baseline / multi-seed synthetic evaluation including sign-flip attacks, and real medical runs (UCI Breast Cancer; MedMNIST when available). We report accuracy, detection, round time, and per-round payloads, position ZKFL-PQ against Beskar-class secure aggregation, and state residual certification gaps (SEAL backends, machine-checked QROM) without over-claiming.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Federated learning (FL)~\cite{mcmahan2017fedavg} keeps raw records at the edge, yet model updates remain vulnerable to gradient inversion~\cite{zhu2019deep}, Byzantine poisoning~\cite{blanchard2017machine}, and HNDL attacks against classical TLS when medical archives outlive RSA/ECC~\cite{mosca2018cybersecurity}. Hospital consortia therefore need (i)~post-quantum (PQ) session confidentiality, (ii)~verifiable rejection of oversized updates, and (iii)~aggregation that does not place a single curious server in sole possession of plaintext gradients.

\paragraph{Why medical FL is special.}
Clinical deployments retain data for years to decades, operate under multi-institutional governance (no single root of trust), and face regulators that increasingly ask how model updates are authenticated and who can decrypt them. A design that only wraps FedAvg in classical TLS is therefore insufficient even if today's quantum computers are not yet cryptanalytically relevant: Harvest-Now-Decrypt-Later adversaries can store ciphertexts today and open them later~\cite{mosca2018cybersecurity}.

\paragraph{Reviewer-driven revision.}

\section{Threat Model and Claim Scope}
\textbf{Adversary capabilities:} (i)~passive network eavesdropper with future quantum computers breaking factoring/DLP; (ii)~up to $f{<}N/2$ Byzantine clients sending arbitrary updates; (iii)~curious aggregation server that sees all protocol messages but does \emph{not} hold $sk$ alone; (iv)~up to $t{-}1$ colluding threshold decryptors.

\textbf{Guarantees we claim:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{PQ transport:} ML-KEM-768 session keys (FIPS~203 Level~3)~\cite{nist_fips203_2024}.
  \item \textbf{Norm soundness (prototype):} updates whose proven vector has $\norm{\tilde{w}}_2>\tau$ fail Unruh/FS verification except with negligible probability under SIS + RO assumptions (classical ROM for FS; Unruh-oriented instantiation shipped).
  \item \textbf{Ciphertext binding:} challenges digest BFV ciphertext bytes; a proof cannot be replayed on a different $\mathbf{ct}$.
  \item \textbf{Aggregator privacy (threshold):} with $sk$ Shamir-shared $(t,n)$ and erased from the server after share generation, the FL server alone cannot decrypt; $\ge t$ parties open via partial decryptions $\mu_i=c_1\star(\lambda_i\cdot s_i)$ without assembling $s$ in one place.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Explicit non-goals:} clinical backdoor robustness; SEAL-certified lattice-estimator reports; machine-checked QROM proofs; directional attacks with $\norm{\cdot}_2\le\tau$ (we \emph{measure} sign-flip failure of pure $\ell_2$ gates). ZKFL-PQ is complementary to Beskar~\cite{zhang2025beskar}, which targets full-stack PQ secure aggregation with assisting nodes and DP.

\section{Protocol Design}

\subsection{Round Structure}
Each round $t$: the server broadcasts $w^{(t)}$. Client $i$ runs local SGD on private $D_i$ to obtain $\Delta w_i\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$, quantizes to $\tilde{w}_i$, encrypts under BFV public key $pk$, proves $\norm{\tilde{w}_i}_2\le\tau$ bound to $\mathbf{ct}_i$, and sends an ML-KEM-wrapped payload. The server verifies proofs, homomorphically sums accepted ciphertexts, and threshold-opens the aggregate (Algorithm~\ref{alg:target}).

\subsection{Quantization}
$\mathrm{Quantize}$ clips coordinates to $[-c,c]$, scales by $s$, and rounds into $\mathbb{Z}_t$. Defaults in the artifact: $c{=}1$, $s{=}10^{3}$. The public threshold $\tau$ applies after quantization. Adaptive rules $\tau^{(t)}=\hat\mu{+}k\hat\sigma$ are compatible; reported runs use fixed $\tau$ (synthetic $\tau{=}5$; medical demo $\tau{=}8$).

\subsection{Bound Norm Language}
\begin{definition}[Bound language]
Let $C=\Commit(\tilde{w};\mathbf{r})=\mathbf{A}(\tilde{w}\|\mathbf{r})\bmod q'$
and $\mathbf{ct}=\Enc_{pk}(\tilde{w};\rho)$. Then
\begin{multline}
\mathcal{L}_{\tau}^{\mathrm{bind}}=
\bigl\{(C,\mathbf{ct},\tau):\exists(\tilde{w},\mathbf{r},\rho)\\
\quad C=\Commit(\tilde{w};\mathbf{r}),\
\mathbf{ct}=\Enc(\tilde{w};\rho),\
\norm{\tilde{w}}_2\le\tau\bigr\}.
\end{multline}
\end{definition}

\paragraph{$\Sigma$-protocol (Lyubashevsky-style)~\cite{lyubashevsky2012lattice}.}
Commit masks $\mathbf{y}$ and message $\tilde{w}$; the Fiat--Shamir challenge is $e=H(C\|\mathbf{ct}\|T\|\tau)$; responses $(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{r}_z)$ satisfy algebraic opening $T+eC\equiv\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{z}\|\mathbf{r}_z)$ and $\norm{\mathbf{z}}_2\le B$. Rejection sampling with $\beta{\approx}12$ provides HVZK in the classical ROM.

\paragraph{Unruh-oriented NIZK.}
Classical FS is not tightly QROM-secure~\cite{kiltz2018qrom}. The artifact implements parallel binary Unruh-style sessions (library default $r{=}128$; medical demos use $r{=}32$--$64$ for CPU latency) with invertible-RO records (\texttt{crypto/qrom\_nizk.py}): each session contributes a bit of the challenge derived from $(C_i,T_i,\mathbf{ct})$, and verification checks RO preimages, challenge consistency, norm bounds, and algebraic openings. This is a \emph{research instantiation} of the transform, not a machine-checked QROM proof.

Additive BFV supports $\Dec(\sum_i\Enc(m_i))=\sum_i m_i$ under a noise budget. Write $e_i$ for fresh encryption noise. After summing $N$ ciphertexts, the noise grows roughly as $N\cdot O(\sigma\sqrt{n})$. For the demo $(N,n,q,t)=(5,512,2^{40},2^{12})$ the scaled plaintext remains recoverable; larger $N$ or $d$ requires RNS moduli and a lattice-estimator report (\texttt{crypto/lattice\_security.py} documents the preset and optionally calls the estimator package).

Secret key coefficients are Shamir-shared among $n{=}3$ hospital decryptors with threshold $t{=}2$, after which the monolithic $sk$ is erased. Fewer than $t$ shares learn nothing about $sk$ in the information-theoretic Shamir sense over the share prime. Opening uses Lagrange-weighted \emph{partial} decryptions $\mu_i=c_1\star(\lambda_i\cdot s_i)$ that are summed with smudging noise: the secret polynomial $s$ is never reconstructed for decryption (a debug-only helper remains for tests).

\paragraph{Layer interaction.}
ML-KEM protects payloads in transit (PQ confidentiality of the session). The ZKP protects the aggregation logic against oversized Byzantine updates. BFV+threshold protects gradient privacy against the aggregation server and against $t{-}1$ decryptors. None of the three layers alone is sufficient for the joint medical threat model; the contribution is their composition with explicit binding.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{ZKFL-PQ round (binding + threshold)}
\label{alg:target}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $w^{(t)}$, $\tau$, KEM $(ek_S,dk_S)$, HE $pk$, decryptor shares
\State Broadcast $w^{(t)}$
\For{each client $i$}
  \State $\tilde{w}\gets\mathrm{Quantize}(\mathrm{LocalSGD}(w^{(t)},D_i))$
  \State $\mathbf{ct}_i\gets\Enc_{pk}(\tilde{w})$; $\pi_i\gets\Prove_{\mathrm{Unruh}}((\mathbf{ct}_i,\tau),\tilde{w})$
  \State send ML-KEM-wrapped $(\mathbf{ct}_i,\pi_i)$
\EndFor
\State Keep $i$ iff $\Verify_{\mathrm{Unruh}}(\pi_i,\mathbf{ct}_i,\tau)=1$
\State $\mathbf{ct}^{\star}\gets\sum_{i\in S}\mathbf{ct}_i$; threshold-open; $w^{(t+1)}\gets w^{(t)}+\mathrm{mean}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{theorem}[Sketch]
Under SIS binding of $\Commit$, RLWE hardness of BFV, and RO modeling of $H$, the NIZK is complete for honest $\norm{\tilde{w}}_2\le\tau$ and sound against oversized witnesses except with $\approx 2^{-r}$ (binary Unruh) plus SIS advantage. Threshold privacy holds against the aggregation server and any $t{-}1$ decryptors.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Findings.}
Large-norm poisoning collapses FedAvg/HE-only; clipping, Multi-Krum, and ZKP-style filters retain accuracy (Table~\ref{tab:res}, Fig.~\ref{fig:acc}--\ref{fig:break}). \textbf{Sign-flip within $\tau$ defeats pure $\ell_2$ gates} (0\% detection; hybrid accuracy collapses like FedAvg), while Multi-Krum still filters by pairwise distance (Fig.~\ref{fig:att}). \paragraph{Ablations (artifact).}

We therefore treat $\tau$ as an operational knob to be calibrated on a validation cohort, not as a universal constant.

\subsection{Security Analysis (Reviewers B/C)}
\paragraph{Completeness.}
Honest clients with $\norm{\tilde{w}}_2\le\tau$ produce Unruh sessions that pass norm and algebraic checks except with rejection-sampling abort probability that decreases as $\sigma_y$ grows; the artifact retries a bounded number of masks.

\paragraph{Soundness against oversized updates.}
If $\norm{\tilde{w}}_2\gg\tau$, then for every Unruh session with challenge bit $1$, $\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{y}+\tilde{w}$ exceeds $B$ with overwhelming probability under the Gaussian mask design, so verification fails. Sessions with bit $0$ do not bind the witness magnitude; soundness therefore relies on the fraction of $1$-challenges ($\approx\tfrac12$) across $r$ repetitions. The library default $r{=}128$ targets a $\approx 128$-bit Unruh soundness class under the binary transform; demos may lower $r$ (e.g., $32$--$64$) for CPU latency at linear proof-size cost.

\paragraph{Binding.}
Because $H$ digests $\mathbf{ct}$, a proof transcript accepting for $\mathbf{ct}$ is invalid for $\mathbf{ct}'\neq\mathbf{ct}$. This closes the split attack (benign proof, malicious ciphertext) flagged against the unbound submission artifact (Reviewer~D).

\paragraph{Privacy.}
BFV semantic security (RLWE) hides individual $\tilde{w}_i$ from parties lacking $sk$. With Shamir $(t,n)$, the aggregation server and any $t{-}1$ decryptors cannot open; $\ge t$ colluding share holders can. We do \emph{not} claim differential privacy; sites needing DP should compose with mechanisms as in Beskar~\cite{zhang2025beskar}.

Beskar remains a more mature full-stack PQ secure-aggregation framework (assisting nodes, dropout, DP composition)~\cite{zhang2025beskar}. Patel~\cite{patel2025pqfl} and Lu et~al.~\cite{lu2024hybrid} study PQ-HE FL without ciphertext-bound norm ZKPs. zkML targets inference proofs rather than FL aggregation~\cite{kang2022zkml}. Statistical defenses such as Krum lack cryptographic soundness but remain strong baselines against low-norm adversaries---as our sign-flip results confirm. ZKFL-PQ's niche is the \emph{explicit} ML-KEM + ciphertext-bound norm ZKP + threshold BFV composition for medical FL, with a public NumPy artifact for reproducibility, not a drop-in Beskar replacement.

\paragraph{Deployment sketch.}
In a hospital consortium, each site runs a client; a neutral coordinator runs verification and homomorphic summation; $t$-of-$n$ key-share holders (e.g., DPO + two hospital CISOs) jointly open aggregates on a schedule. Daily/weekly training cadence absorbs the observed $\sim$20$\times$ CPU overhead of the hybrid stack relative to plaintext FedAvg on the synthetic workload. For imaging-scale $d$ (MedMNIST / radiology CNNs), the same protocol applies but HE chunk counts and Unruh proof sizes grow with $d$; we therefore recommend starting with tabular or compact models (as in the UCI demo) when validating the crypto pipeline before scaling encoders.

\paragraph{Unruh session verify (artifact).}
For each repetition $i\in[r]$ the verifier checks: (1)~SHA3$(\rho_i)=h_i$ (invertible-RO record); (2)~challenge bit $c_i$ matches $H(\mathrm{stmt}\|\{T_j\})$; (3)~$\norm{\mathbf{z}_i}_2\le B$; (4)~$\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{z}_i\|\mathbf{r}_{z,i})\equiv T_i+c_i C_i\pmod{q'}$. Failure of any check rejects the client update. Proof size scales linearly in $r$ and $d$; demos may use $r{<}128$ as a latency/soundness compromise for workshop reproduction on CPU while keeping the library default at $r{=}128$.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
All numbers in Sec.~V are produced by pinned scripts in~\cite{zkflcode}: \texttt{experiments/run\_baselines.py} (synthetic multi-seed) and \texttt{experiments/run\_target\_protocol.py} (UCI + threshold + Unruh + full-vector HE). Seeds, hyperparameters, and JSON outputs are committed alongside the code so EDAS reviewers can re-run without private data access.

\section{Limitations and Residual Roadmap}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item[\checkmark] Binding, trusted-Boolean removal, full-vector HE, threshold BFV with partial decrypt (no reconstructed $sk$), Unruh NIZK ($r{=}128$ default), Classic-128 presets, UCI/MedMNIST demos, multi-baseline/sign-flip study.
  \item SEAL/OpenFHE production backend (NumPy stack remains a research encoder); plug-in lattice-estimator certificates beyond the shipped reporter.
  \item Dedicated Enc-consistency gadget for encryption randomness $\rho$ (today: transcript binding).
  \item Larger $N$, longer $T$, full-resolution imaging workloads without feature projection, machine-checked QROM.
\end{enumerate}

These residuals are \emph{engineering and proof-engineering} items; they do not undo the reviewer-facing fixes already landed in the artifact. A fair camera-ready reading is: the protocol is now coherently specified and implemented; certification-grade cryptography remains future work.

\paragraph{Worked round (medical demo numbers).}
On UCI Breast Cancer with $d{=}1554$, $n{=}512$ yields $\lceil d/n\rceil{=}4$ BFV ciphertexts per client. With $N{=}5$ honest clients, uplink is dominated by $5\times 4$ ciphertext pairs ($\approx 1.7$\,MB/round before rejection). After the Byzantine client is rejected, payload drops to $\approx 1.4$\,MB. End-to-end wall time $\approx 4$--$5$\,s/round on a laptop CPU (NumPy), acceptable for nightly hospital jobs. Accuracy trajectory $88.6\%\!\rightarrow\!93.0\%\!\rightarrow\!93.9\%$ shows learning continues under threshold decryption and Unruh verification.

\paragraph{Takeaway for eHPWAS/WiMob.}
The wireless/mobile systems angle is not ``faster ML'', but \emph{budgetable} PQ verifiable aggregation: megabytes per round, seconds of CPU, and explicit trust anchors (KEM, ZKP, threshold shares) that match multi-hospital governance. That is the systems story reviewers asked to see beyond a single synthetic plot.
